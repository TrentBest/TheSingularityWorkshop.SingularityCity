# Vigilantes & Civic Justice — the line between protection and private power

**Owner:** Singularity City · **LOD:** 2 civic / character specialization · **Status:** authored domain sourcebook; no vigilante combat or enforcement runtime is implied.

A vigilante is a person or group that claims to protect a community or deliver justice outside, or in conflict with, recognized public authority. The role may be heroic, reckless, corrupt, desperate, theatrical, misunderstood or predatory. A mask does not establish virtue. A good intention does not create lawful authority.

Vigilantes are valuable fictional actors because they expose the gap between public safety, institutional trust, fear, injustice and community self-organization. The model should allow players to become one, oppose one, investigate one, protect one, or discover that a celebrated local guardian is causing harm.

## 1. Define the authority boundary

Keep these roles distinct:
- **Bystander:** observes or reports an event.
- **Good Samaritan:** offers immediate assistance within capability and context.
- **Community responder:** participates in organized mutual aid or lawful neighborhood support.
- **Licensed security:** acts under a defined contract and jurisdiction, not general public authority.
- **Public officer:** exercises powers granted by law and accountable to an institution.
- **Vigilante:** self-appoints or claims authority to investigate, punish, detain or enforce outside recognized process.
- **Insurgent or resistance actor:** challenges the governing order; motives and legitimacy remain contextual.
- **Predatory actor:** exploits fear or claims of protection to control, extort or harm others.

These are roles in a situation, not permanent moral identities. A character can move between them, and institutions can be legitimate in one context and abusive in another.

## 2. Why a vigilante emerges

Possible causes include:
- slow, absent, inaccessible or distrusted institutions;
- repeated harm with no credible remedy;
- an emergency beyond available response capacity;
- community mutual aid that becomes personalized around a charismatic figure;
- a perceived injustice, real or mistaken;
- political conflict, factional rivalry or propaganda;
- a desire for recognition, revenge, control or profit;
- a deliberate effort to undermine public institutions.

Record what the character believes, what evidence they possess, who supports them, who is harmed, and which alternatives were available. Do not treat the character's justification as objective truth.

## 3. Masks, identities and public knowledge

A mask or alias may protect a character's privacy, create a symbol, conceal affiliation or enable intimidation. Keep actual identity, public persona, rumor, suspected identity and verified identity separate. Different observers can hold different beliefs. Revelation should require an authored event or justified discovery; the simulation must not make every NPC omniscient.

Identity disclosure can carry risk. Do not make private identity automatically public merely because a player selects a vigilante role. Trusted host systems—not costume, reputation or roleplay—control real account identity and permissions.

## 4. Community response and legitimacy

Residents may celebrate, fear, tolerate, condemn, imitate, report, shelter, challenge or organize against a vigilante. Reactions vary across neighborhoods and over time. A loud supporter is not proof of majority support; a poll is not a referendum; an online rumor is not evidence.

Measure separate dimensions where useful: perceived safety, trust in institutions, fear, reported harm, willingness to cooperate, confidence in due process and actual incident outcomes. Avoid collapsing these into one popularity or morality score.

A vigilante may prevent an immediate harm yet cause a wrongful accusation, injury, unlawful detention, retaliation or escalation. The record must preserve both the claimed benefit and the costs to affected people.

## 5. Player choices and consequences

A player may choose to:
- provide aid, witness information or escort someone to safety;
- organize lawful mutual aid or neighborhood preparedness;
- report misconduct and support independent oversight;
- investigate a fictional mystery within the scenario's rules;
- assume a masked protector identity in a fictional setting;
- refuse, expose or oppose a vigilante's methods;
- seek a court remedy, restorative process or public inquiry;
- negotiate an exit, amnesty or reintegration where the polity permits.

Consequences should arise from observable actions, context and evidence—not a hidden alignment meter. The city records incidents and relationships; Law Enforcement & Public Safety owns official response; Justice owns adjudication and remedies; Governance owns institutional reform; Economy owns any compensation or transfer of value.

This book describes fictional narrative choices, not real-world tactics for surveillance, apprehension, evasion, weapons, coercion or violence.

## 6. Accountability and harm

For any incident, keep distinct:
- allegation and source;
- direct observation and witness account;
- physical or documentary evidence and provenance;
- action taken and authority claimed;
- people affected and their account of harm;
- immediate safety outcome;
- investigation and findings;
- legal determination, if any;
- remedy, restitution or appeal;
- unresolved uncertainty and later correction.

A vigilante's public reputation cannot substitute for a finding. A mistaken accusation must be correctable. A victim's needs do not disappear when a dramatic confrontation ends.

## 8. Validation scenarios

- A masked helper assists during a flood, then returns to ordinary civic life.
- A celebrated vigilante wrongly identifies a resident; evidence corrects the public story.
- A neighborhood patrol becomes a lawful mutual-aid organization with limited authority.
- A vigilante exposes corruption but also compromises evidence and harms an innocent person.
- A player refuses vigilantism and pursues oversight and court remedies.
- A community supports a protector while another neighborhood fears them.
- An official abuses authority; lawful protest and independent investigation remain available.

## 9. Implementation boundary

This is a fictional character and civic-role sourcebook. It does not authorize real-world vigilantism, create legal powers, implement an enforcement service, or provide operational instruction for violence or coercion. A playable role must remain bounded by the setting's declared law, player safety controls and the consequences experienced by other characters.
